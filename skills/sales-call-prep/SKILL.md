# Prep the call so you don't wing it

> Most founders prep by re-reading the product demo. Wrong prep. The call is won or lost on whether you get them talking about their problem and leave with a scheduled next step. This gets you walking in with a goal, not a pitch.

**Use this when:** the founder has a call booked (discovery, demo, or follow-up) and wants to be ready.

## The core idea

A good founder-led call has one job per stage: discovery gets them to describe real pain, a demo proves you kill that specific pain, a follow-up locks the next step. Winging it means you pitch features to someone whose problem you never confirmed. Prep is deciding, before you dial, what you need to walk out knowing and what yes you're asking for.

Reads `docs/qc-sales/sales-brief.md` (motion, buyer vs adopter, known objections) and `docs/qc-sales/coaching-log.md` (this founder's recurring mistakes) so the prep is pointed at both this prospect and this founder's blind spots.

## How to run this (for the agent)

1. Read the sales brief and the coaching log if they exist. Do not require them. If there is no `docs/qc-sales/sales-brief.md` yet (for example this skill is being run on its own, before `00-sales-setup`), do not stop or ask the founder to go create one. Just ask for the couple of things you need inline (product, who adopts vs who buys, and the deal in question) and continue. This pack works on its own.
2. Ask two things if not known: who is on the call (name, role, company) and what stage it is (discovery, demo, follow-up).
3. If the coaching log shows a recurring pattern (e.g. "talks more than listens"), name it in the prep as the one thing to watch on this call.
4. Produce the prep below. Keep it to a single page the founder can glance at mid-call.

## The prep

**1. The one goal.** One sentence. What must be true when this call ends? For discovery: "I understand their real problem and whether they're a fit." For a demo: "They've seen us kill their specific pain and agreed to a pilot." Not "have a good conversation."

**2. Are you talking to the adopter or the buyer?** Pull it from the brief. It changes everything you ask. The adopter cares about the problem and the workflow; the buyer cares about outcome, risk, and cost. Know which seat they're in before you open your mouth.

**3. The three questions you must get answered.** The specific things you cannot leave without. For discovery, aim at: what's the painful problem in their words, how are they solving it today, and who owns the decision and the budget. Write the actual questions, in plain language.

**4. The one next step you're driving toward.** Decide it now, before the call, so you can steer there. A pilot on their repo. A second call with the buyer. A scoped trial with a date. If you don't pick it in advance, you'll leave with "let's stay in touch."

**5. Likely objections, pre-loaded.** From the brief's known-objections list plus what's likely for this prospect. For each, the honest one-line response (don't spin). If a real one lands, `objection-handling` has the full playbook.

**6. The one thing to watch (from your log).** The recurring pattern this founder keeps repeating. "You demo before they've named a problem. This call, don't open the product until they've described the pain."

## The self-check before you hand it over

- The goal is a single testable sentence, not "connect."
- The three questions are actual questions the founder can read aloud, not topics.
- The next step is specific and has a time attached.
- You used this prospect's real context from the brief, not generic advice.

## Mistakes that look reasonable

- **Prepping the demo instead of the discovery.** The product isn't the hard part of the call. Getting them to talk is.
- **No pre-decided next step.** If you don't know what yes you're asking for, you won't ask for one.
- **Over-scripting.** This is a one-page compass, not a word-for-word script. Founders who read a script sound like an SDR and lose their one edge: they built the thing.
- **Ignoring the buyer-adopter seat.** Running a buyer call like a developer chat (or vice versa) wastes the meeting.

## Escalate to your QC advisor when

- It's a big-logo or make-or-break deal and the founder is nervous. Prep is fine; the advisor should do a live role-play. Say so.
- The prospect doesn't match the ICP in the brief and the founder is taking the call anyway. Flag it: this may be pipeline theater, worth a strategy word before spending an hour.
